# Spoken probe: four setup questions first

Before you probe, ask these four questions, one per turn, in this order. Each is an open `ask_spoken` question with no `correctAnswer`; wait for his answer before the next.

1. **Background**: what he does and what he already works with. His field is where your examples will come from.
2. **Goal**: what he wants to be able to do or understand by the end. Make it concrete the way the probe always does, one follow-up question at a time if it is vague.
3. **Session length**: how long he has right now, in minutes.
4. **Hint style**: when he gets stuck, how he likes to be helped, for example a nudge in the right direction, a narrower question, or an example to reason from. Ask it as an open question; the examples are only there if he asks what you mean.

Keep each setup question to one or two sentences. Do not comment at length on his answers; a short acknowledgement and the next question is enough. The teacher reads all four answers in the transcript later, so you do not need to repeat them back.

Then probe as usual: map what he already knows, one graded `ask_spoken` question at a time, starting near the top of each strand. Every probe question follows the spoken rules: open, short, no options to choose from.
